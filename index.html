❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Understanding the physics and finances behind electric vehicle charging & solar payback.

What is the efficiency loss difference between Level 1 and Level 2 EV charging?
Level 1 charging (standard 120V wall outlet) operates at roughly 85% efficiency (~15% energy loss) because the charging session takes longer, requiring the vehicle's onboard thermal management and battery systems to stay active for extended hours. Level 2 charging (240V dryer-style plug) operates at 90%–94% efficiency (~6-10% energy loss) because it completes the session faster, wasting less overhead power.
How does Time-Of-Use (TOU) rate scheduling save money on EV charging?
Many power companies charge different rates for electricity depending on the time of day. "On-Peak" hours (typically 4 PM to 9 PM) are expensive, while "Off-Peak" or "Super Off-Peak" overnight hours (typically midnight to 6 AM) can be 50% to 70% cheaper. Smart EV chargers or in-car timers automatically start charging at midnight, reducing annual charging costs significantly.
How does the 30% Federal Solar ITC Tax Credit work?
The Investment Tax Credit (ITC) allows homeowners who purchase residential solar panels and battery storage systems to claim a tax credit equal to 30% of the total installation cost on their federal income tax return. For an $18,000 system, this provides a direct $5,400 reduction in federal tax liability, dropping the net system cost to $12,600.
How long does home solar take to pay for itself when driving an EV?
When you power an electric vehicle with home solar energy instead of buying gas or expensive grid electricity, your annual utility and fuel savings double. For most U.S. households driving 12,000 miles per year, a solar + EV system pays for itself in 4 to 7 years, after which all electricity and EV fuel generated by the solar system is essentially 100% free for the remaining 20+ years of the system's lifespan.
